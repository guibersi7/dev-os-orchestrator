Marks

six directions · 1j–1o

Every mark is built from squares, circles and bars only — it survives a 16px favicon, a menu-bar icon and a monochrome print. Each card shows the mark at 64px and at 20px, because the small size is the one that decides.

1j the queue

Three bars, ranked. The most literal expression of the product: not a list, a priority. Reads instantly at 16px.

1k the event

A square inside a circle: the normalizer. Any shape that arrives leaves as the same shape. Abstract enough to age well.

1l converge

Many sources on the left, one spine on the right. The weakest at small sizes — the dots close up. Keep only if the wordmark carries the load.

1m the chain

Dependency: your review, the issue, the release. Diagonal gives it motion, which no other mark here has.

1n first light

The morning, literally. Warm and human — the risk is that a sunrise says wellness app, not engineering tool.

1o the cut

A single slash — the cut through noise. Quietest of the six, and the most at home next to a terminal. Also the least distinctive.

My recommendation

Upstream (1d) with the queue mark (1j). The name is native to the vocabulary engineers already use, it points at the product's real job — finding where work originates and where it is stuck — and it survives the company growing past GitHub. The mark says “ranked”, which is the one promise every screen keeps.

Second choice: Foreground (1g) — a better name, a harder domain. Keep “Developer OS” as the category line under whichever name wins: “Upstream · the developer OS”.
